Phone DBMS Benchmarks

Timings of the public database_* calls on the target hardware, measured through the journal and the SD storage layer. Each call also reports how many 512 B sectors it read and wrote. This page reports measurements only; it does not recommend changes.

MCU STM32H723ZG @ 520 MHz Storage microSD, SDMMC 4-bit @ 6 MHz Build -Os, I-cache on, D-cache off Capacity 10,000 contacts

Main findings

  • SD writes dominate every update. Each sector read costs about 0.45 ms and each write about 3 ms; the raw transfer is only ~0.17 ms. A contact add makes 7 writes (22–28 ms), a message add 12–14 (42–48 ms), a call add 4.8 (14–17 ms).
  • Boot time grows faster than the contact count. database_init() takes 1.5 s at 1,000 contacts and 36.6 s at 10,000 (76,045 sector reads). Most of the reads come from rebuilding the name index: the heapsort reads both contacts from SD whenever two entries share an 8-byte key.
  • Reading back into a long chat grows linearly with the offset. In a 1,000-message chat the newest 20 messages take 3.9 ms and the oldest 20 take 155 ms. Removing a 100-message chat takes 571 ms.
  • Lookups are fast. contact_get takes ~0.9 ms (2 reads), a missed lookup under 1 µs, a prefix seek under 3 µs. Recent-message and recent-call pages take 2–6 ms.
  • Format takes 30.7 s, almost all of it clearing the two 5,000-sector history rings.

Method

  • The timed region is one API call. Times come from the DWT cycle counter, with the 1 ms HAL tick for regions of 4 s or more. Interrupts stay enabled, because the SD driver waits on DMA through the scheduler.
  • Every database is formatted, then filled through the public API. Operations that would change the stored set (add, remove, update) are undone untimed, so each checkpoint keeps a known state.
  • 100 timed repetitions after 10 warm-up per point on the target. Fill rows time every add, and their percentiles use at most 1,000 evenly spaced samples.
  • Contacts have unique phone numbers and "First Last" names drawn from 1,280 combinations. Names repeat as real names do; this generator probably produces more key ties than real names would.
  • The code lives in source/benchmark/database/. The firmware is linked with -Wl,--wrap=DB_Init, so main.c is unchanged. Host runs on a RAM-backed store exist for checking the code path but are not comparable and are not shown here.

3 · Calls

1,000 contacts. 80% of callers are known contacts and 20% are new numbers. A known caller costs 2 extra reads because the contact is copied into the call entry. Both adds stay flat from 100 calls to after the 25,000-entry ring wraps.

The two long fills (20,000 and 5,000 back-to-back adds) ran at a 27.4 ms median, against ~15 ms everywhere else, with identical sector counts. Per-call times look bimodal, which would fit a card-side effect under sustained writing. This was not verified.

4 · Init and recovery

database_init() recovers the journal and rebuilds the hash table, chat order, name index and ring buffer heads. At 10,000 contacts the name index rebuild accounts for roughly 64,000 of the 76,045 reads. The last row cuts the power after the first 6 writes of a message add. Recovery restored the correct counts in all 5 repetitions and added no I/O.

Reproduce

On the NUCLEO-H723ZG with an SD card (the benchmark formats the card): scripts/run_target.sh <contacts|messages|calls|init> Release. Tables come from scripts/summarize.py, and scripts/compare.py compares two runs. Raw CSVs and run metadata are in source/benchmark/database/results/.
